## 1. The fight

In the last decades of the nineteenth century, physics was fought over notation the way
we now fight over interpretations of quantum mechanics. James Clerk Maxwell's
*Treatise on Electricity and Magnetism* (1873) carried the electromagnetic field in the
language of quaternions — the four-dimensional numbers William Rowan Hamilton had carved
into a bridge in 1843. Maxwell died in 1879, and within fifteen years his equations had
been rewritten by Oliver Heaviside (and, in parallel, Willard Gibbs) into the
three-dimensional vector calculus we all learned: four tidy vector equations, div and
curl, no quaternion in sight.

Peter Guthrie Tait — Hamilton's champion, the "quaternionist" — called the vectorists'
creation "a sort of hermaphrodite monster." Heaviside answered in kind. The vectorists
won. The textbooks today are their victory monument. [RECALLED — the polemics and dates
need source receipts before this section hardens.]

But a question was left on the battlefield, and it was never answered, only abandoned:
**what was amputated in the reduction?**
